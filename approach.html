The SCFL approach

SCFL is the measurement architecture for a proposed construct. The construct says what coherence is made of; SCFL is how it is observed, quantified, challenged and potentially falsified.

The construct: three components

Each component answers a different question about how relationships hold one another in place, and each must be defined without using the word coherence, its presumed consequences, or the procedure used to detect it.

I

Integration

To what extent do the parts participate in a relational organization that constitutes a whole?

A network can be fully connected while its relationships are redundant, fragmented, or unable to support the larger structure. Integration is the property that separates a whole from mere connectivity.

Km

Mutual constraint

What makes a set of relationships mutually determinate rather than merely coexisting?

In a mutually constrained structure, a change in one relationship alters what the others can be. This is the component pairwise measures cannot capture (see the measurement problem).

Re

Recoverability

Does the structure retain what it needs to restore its organization after perturbation?

Defined as a capacity, not an outcome, and measured without knowing whether recovery later occurred. Observed recovery stays an independent outcome it can be tested against.

Comparing systems without a single score

Adding the three components into one number would let them substitute for one another: high integration could offset a complete absence of mutual constraint. The working definition says that should not happen.

So systems are compared by dominance. System A is more coherent than system B only if it is at least as high on all three components and higher on at least one. If each is higher on something, they are left unranked. Scalar rules (additive, geometric mean, weakest link) are kept as rivals to be tested with weights fixed in advance.

CCV(t) estimates what a system can still do, as distinct from what it is currently doing. It has not yet been validated against observed recovery.

From observation to prediction

A measurement can reveal structural change without being a prediction. Keeping these steps apart stops a useful predictor from being mistaken for proof of what it measures.

  1. Observation

    Recording what the system’s data shows, on data that already exists.

  2. Measurement

    Turning observations into defined, reproducible quantities.

  3. State characterization

    Describing the structure those quantities reveal at a given time.

  4. Change detection

    Identifying when and how that structure changes.

  5. Prediction

    Whether measured change precedes or constrains outcomes. Pursued only after measurement is established.

SCFL’s activityDownstream, only where separately validated

Because recoverability is defined as a capacity, its relationship to observed recovery can be tested. Four results are possible: it strongly predicts recovery; it does so only in some domains; recovery proves independent of it; or an apparent relationship disappears under stronger controls. Only the first supports the construct, and even that would not establish a general law.

Three kinds of terms

Words like drift, rupture and stress have everyday meanings and technical counterparts. The program keeps three registers apart.

  • Everyday terms, in lowercase, describe phenomena and make no technical commitment.
  • Measurement terms name quantities SCFL reports, such as C(t), Cmin and CCV(t).
  • Operator terms, in capitals, name the six frozen operators: DRIFT, SHIFT, RUPTURE, KINETIC, QUENCH and STRESS. They are defined in the SCFL canon, not on this site.

Not an early warning system

Early warning research shows that systems nearing a tipping point recover more slowly from small disturbances. That work characterizes proximity to a transition. SCFL characterizes whether relationships hold one another in place, and whether the structure retains what it needs to recover.

Its primary object is the system’s state space and relational structure. Its primary activity is measurement and characterization, not warning generation.
