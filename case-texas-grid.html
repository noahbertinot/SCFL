Texas grid, February 2021

Winter Storm Uri brought the Texas grid within minutes of a collapse that could have lasted weeks. The program reads the night as the end of a long structural path rather than a five-minute emergency.

Public record
Source: KUT, 24 February 2021

What happened

In the early morning of 15 February 2021, the frequency of the ERCOT grid dropped below 59.4 hertz at about 1:51 a.m. ERCOT officials said a cascading failure would have begun if the frequency had stayed below that threshold for nine minutes or more, and that the grid came within 4 minutes and 37 seconds of that point.

ERCOT’s chief executive told its board that a blackout of the system would have lasted “an indeterminate amount of time,” and suggested outages could have persisted for weeks or longer.

Narrative reading
From the film below

The five minutes were the siren

The program’s reading is that the minutes on 15 February were the end of the story, not the failure itself. The failure was the long path that put the operators in those last minutes: weatherization, fuel that could not move in the cold, and a winter peak the plan did not carry.

An early warning clock starts when frequency is already falling. SCFL is designed to measure the trajectory before that, from drift to rupture.

Exploratory
Single event, retrospective

A retrospective reading

The program reports that, on a retrospective plate of February 2021, the grid followed a regime path from stable to breach, with a critical state about seventy-two hours before the loss was expressed. Later ERCOT stress has been read on high-frequency operational data.

Related deposits: Uri Precursor Window and Operator Decision Rule and The SCFL: A Real-Time Measurement Instrument Validated on ERCOT Grid Stress Events. Titles are the authors’. A deposit is not peer review.

Exploratory
Documented in the Operators Test and Validation Notebook

A hard check

To see whether the reading was real, the program ran it against synthetic data built to keep the ordinary shape of that night while removing the feature thought to be the signal. The Uri reading held.

The same check took something back. Four earthquake sequences that had looked like the same kind of reading turned out to be an artifact of how fast the event count was changing. That result was withdrawn and kept in the record so the miss stays visible. A volcanic eruption held one reading, on that eruption alone.

What “held” means here: this night, not the next event.

Pre-registered

The next test

A pre-registration for a test of ERCOT self-replenishment thresholds has been deposited: CCV_E Pre-registration (ERCOT Self-Replenishment Threshold Test). Fixing hypotheses and decision rules before the data are computed is what keeps a retrospective reading from becoming a story fitted after the fact.

Limits. One event, read after the fact, not yet replicated outside the program. In the program’s own words: we know one night well; we do not yet know the next. SCFL does not replace the operating standards and planning tools grid operators already use.
